Firefly System

Firefly is an outdoor LED display built from 1 ft panels in four block sizes. Each block carries its own power supply and control card in one slim L hub, and its panels hang on pins with no tools. This page covers the whole product: the panel, hub and wiring, both metal frames (Rib in folded aluminum, Hoop in roll-formed steel), how blocks join and mount, and a full set of fabrication drawings.

The system at a glance

Four blocks, every wall size

Every block is its own part and hangs one way only: the 2 x 3 is 2 high and 3 wide, the 3 x 2 is 3 high and 2 wide. Walls are laid out as the block planner does: each side splits into 3 ft lanes with one or two 2 ft lanes for any leftover, and every crossing of a row and a column is one block, so every size from 2 ft up builds with the fewest blocks.

Frame weights are bare frames before powder coat. A complete block adds about 3 lb per panel and 5.5 lb for the hub. Joint bolts are the M8s each block brings: one down per foot of width and two across at each side station above the logo field.

The planner walls

Conventional is one supply and one card per 2 sq ft cabinet. Circuits are 20 A at 120 V for 6 mm pitch, counted along the hub to hub chain (see Wiring and power).

The panel and its gravity lock

Push it in, let go, and gravity holds it. No tools and no latch. Sheet C-1.

  1. Each panel is 7/8 in deep: an LED face sealed with silicone potting on a thin aluminum back tray, with one sealed connector at the center of its back and a small 48 V to 5 V converter. There is no gasket.
  2. Four small plastic bosses (glass filled, UV stabilized) stand off its back, one per pin, each with a round end and softened edges. Each holds a 6 mm 316 stainless pin pointing out to the side: two 70 mm below the panel top and two 60 mm above its bottom.
  3. The U rails open toward the panels. The leg next to the bosses has a J groove for each pin: straight back from the front edge, then down 1/2 in into a pocket. On Rib the rails fold out of the walls and seam channels; on Hoop they are rolled and punched into the ring.
  4. To hang a panel, plug its drop tap into the center of its back, line the four pins up with their grooves, push it in and let go. It drops 1/2 in and stays there. The lip in front of each pocket holds it against wind suction.
  5. It comes out only by lifting it 1/2 in first, then pulling it toward you. Panels touch edge to edge, so they go on from the bottom row up and come off from the top down; to swap one, lift out the panels above it in its column first.
  6. The far side of each U hides the pins from the side and its closed back hides them from behind. Inside a block one seam rail carries both neighboring columns; at a block seam the two side walls touch, each with its own U.

Panel view in the 3D model cuts the side wall away at the upper pin, and Play hangs a panel on its pins.

The L hub

One supply and one card per block, in an L that passes through the opening one panel leaves.

The hub sits behind the top right panel seen from the front, the top left seen from the back: the arm across the top, the leg down the outer side, 1.6 in thick, with one cable out of the foot of the leg. The arm is 9.8 in long and 4.9 in tall and the leg 4.1 in wide, 9 in overall, so it passes the 10.4 in opening with the panel out.

Inside

The supply is split around the inside corner: its 48 V output stage and the harness eFuse sit in the arm, the PFC front end and bulk capacitors at the top of the leg, and the receiving card below them, above the cable gland at the foot. The Cirrus IN and OUT face back, side by side in the middle of the arm. Turn on Inside hub in Block view to see the boards.

Mounting and heat

Rib hangs the hub from its top flange and Hoop tilts it on a hinge shelf, both shown below; neither fixes anything to a side wall. The hub sits in the open 6 in cavity behind the panels, with vents in every frame wall above the logo field.

Rib: the hub hangs from the top

One hook, one spring pin, nothing on the side walls. Sheet R-5.

Side view through the spring pin, from the outer side, front to the right.

A small folded hanger is riveted inside the top back flange, so the flange still takes angle iron and nothing touches a side wall. A stainless hook on top of the hub's arm hangs on the hanger's lip, right over the hub's center of gravity, so the hub hangs plumb, and a spring pin on the hook snaps through the lip.

With the panel out, pull the orange knob and lift the hub 1/2 in so the hook clears the lip. Ease it forward a little and lower it, so the hook passes under the top wall's front lip, and bring it out the front. The IN, OUT and harness stay plugged in on service loops until the hub is out.

Hoop: the hub tilts out

Two hinge pins below, one spring latch above. Sheet H-5.

Side view from the outer side with the side wall left off, front to the right. Dashed: the hub tilted 38 degrees.

A hinge shelf clinched to the seam rail carries two stainless hinge pins in its ears, and two hooks at the foot of the hub's leg sit on them. A spring latch on a strap clinched under the top flange catches the hub's arm. Nothing touches a side wall.

With the panel out, press the latch and the hub tilts forward 38 degrees on the pins, bringing the Cirrus IN and OUT on its back up into reach. Lift it 1/2 in off the pins and bring it out the front.

Wiring and power

One cable inside each block and one Cirrus panel cable between blocks.

Inside a block

One cable leaves the hub through a gland at the foot of its leg and runs down the outer side, the left seen from the back. A T feeds each upper row and the bottom row takes a plain turn, so a 3 x 3 has two Ts and a turn. Every row cable runs 4 in below the panel centers, and every panel gets the same overmolded drop tap with a short tail up into the connector at the center of its back. Data runs along each row, and a tap passes it on when its panel is out.

A 48 V harness

The supply makes 48 V, so the cable stays about 1/2 in thick at the hub. Each panel has its own small converter to 5 V with its own fuse, so a faulty panel drops out alone, and one eFuse in the hub protects the whole harness.

Between blocks

The IN and OUT on each hub match the Cirrus outdoor panel connectors, power and data in one cable: IN on the right and OUT on the left seen from the back. One standard Cirrus panel cable runs from each hub's OUT to the next hub's IN. AC passes through a 20 A bus in each hub and the card passes the data on, so the Cirrus controller lead and power injectors work as they do today.

  • Cirrus IN (green) and OUT (orange) on the hub
  • Standard Cirrus panel cable, OUT to the next IN
  • Where a circuit starts: the controller lead and circuit 1 at the first block, a power injector for each one after
  • Shading changes with each circuit

Blocks on one 20 A circuit

Whole 3 x 3 blocks per circuit, from the Cirrus panel figures; 4 mm is an estimate. Mixed walls count by area, 9 sq ft per 3 x 3.

What both frames share

These are the shared interfaces, so panels, hubs, mounting steel and install steps stay the same whichever frame is built, and Rib and Hoop blocks join each other.

  1. 6 in deep sides on all four edges. With the 7/8 in panel the screen is 6 7/8 in deep, mid pack for the industry (Daktronics GS6 is 5 in, Watchfire signs 5 to 8 in, Optec 6.7 in).
  2. A 2 in back flange on all four edges. 7/16 in holes every 2 in on its centerline, 1 in in from every block edge, with a lip on the inside edge for stiffness. Every planner wall puts block edges on whole feet, so every hole on the wall lands on one 2 in grid.
  3. A logo field on every side wall. The lower 16 in of each side has no holes, slots, rivets or fasteners. The Cirrus logo runs vertically up from the bottom corner, so on a finished screen it sits low on both outer sides where people passing on the street see it. Every block has the field, so any block can be a corner.
  4. Two lines on every wall, a joint every foot. A front line 1 1/2 in back from the front edge and a back line 4 3/8 in back. The top has a tapered pin on the front line and an M8 nut on the back line in the middle of every foot; the bottom has a round socket and an M8 hole at the same spots. The right side (seen from the front) has M8 nuts on both lines in the middle of every foot above the logo field, and the left side has M8 holes at the same spots.
  5. Drop straight down, bolt down, bolt across. A leveled base rail with the same pins starts the first row. Every block drops straight down onto the pins below it, so it lands in place with nothing to slide, then an M8 in every foot bolts it down and two M8s in every foot of its side seam bolt it to its neighbor. Every bolt is the same M8 x 16.
  6. The same gravity lock. 20 mm U rails, J grooves and a 1/2 in drop on both, so the same panels hang in either frame.
  7. The L hub in the top right bay seen from the front, the top left seen from the back, with one cable out of the foot of its leg and the Cirrus IN and OUT on its back. Nothing that holds it is fixed to a side wall, and its leads leave through the open back, so neither frame has a cable hole.

The logo field

The lower 16 in of each side stays solid. The logo reads up the side.

The logo is 11 in long and 3.4 in tall, starting 1 in above the bottom edge and 1.6 in back from the front edge. Every block carries the field, and the two bottom corner blocks of a screen show the logo: printed or applied on Rib, and debossed into every Hoop strip by the punch line, filled white on the frames ordered for the bottom corners.

Vents are fine everywhere else, and both frames use the same 11 mm round holes on a 22 mm pitch.

How blocks align and join

Pins in front, bolts behind, the way Cirrus frames join today: bolts from inside one block into nuts mounted in the next. Every block drops straight down onto pins in the block or base rail below it, bolts down, then bolts across to its neighbor. The same parts sit at the same spots on every block, Rib or Hoop, so any block joins any other. Sheet C-2.

  • Tapered pin
  • Round socket
  • M8 nut, top and right side
  • M8 hole, bottom and left side
  • Logo field, no holes

Why it lines up

Each pin sits in a round socket with 0.1 mm of play, so a block lands within about 0.25 mm of where it belongs, side to side and front to back, with nothing to adjust. The first row lands on the base rail's pins, which sit on the same 1 ft spacing, so every block in every row is set by the one below it. No block is ever slid sideways into place, and the side M8s only have to close the seam.

Why it holds

The M8 in every foot pulls each block down onto the one below and carries the wind across the row seams. Two M8s in every foot of a side seam, one on each line, clamp the walls of side by side blocks together, so the seam acts as one piece front and back. The pins take the shear, so no bolt ever has to hold a block in position.

Install order

Level the base rail on its seats. Hang the frames empty, row by row from the bottom: drop each one onto its pins, run its M8s down from inside, then bolt it to the block beside it. The side M8s always go in from inside the right hand block (seen from the front) into the nuts of the left hand one, so they work whichever way the row is hung. Hang the panels last. One 13 mm socket does every joint, by hand.

Mounting: one hole grid for every wall

Every hole on every block lands on one 2 in grid, so the steel lines up wherever the building needs it. Pick a wall from the planner. Sheets C-3 and C-4.

    Why the holes line up

    Holes sit 1 in in from every block edge and every 2 in along it. The planner puts every block edge on a whole foot, so across a whole wall the holes fall on odd inches, both ways. A hole line along a row seam runs unbroken from one end of the wall to the other, and so does one up a column seam. At any seam the two blocks' lines sit 2 in apart, so a clip or a 4 in angle catches both.

    Angle iron

    A 2 x 2 x 3/16 angle bolts straight to the flange with 3/8 in bolts every 6 in, no clips: along the bottom hole line of every row and the top line of the wall. Rows are 2 or 3 ft tall, so the angles are never more than 3 ft apart, inside the 4 ft rule. Any other hole line works too.

    Square tube

    4 in tubes run at mid row on galvanized steel clips bolted into the side flange holes: a 2 in clip at each end of the wall and a 4 in clip on every side seam, taking a hole in each block. Each clip bolts to the tube with one 5/8 in bolt, or welds to it.

    Base rail

    A 6 x 2 x 1/4 in aluminum tube runs under the first row on leveled seats at the uprights, no more than 5 ft apart. It has the same pin and tapped M8 in every foot as the top of a block, so the bottom row bolts down to it the way every row bolts to the one below.

    Utility holes

    Every hole the steel doesn't use takes a cable tie, a P clip or a conduit strap, so the chain cables, a disconnect or a sensor can mount anywhere round the back.

    Strength: one support every 4 ft

    Load case: 50 psf of wind (about 120 mph, exposure C) on a screen held every 4 ft of height, treated as a simple 4 ft span, the worst case. On the planner walls the steel is never more than 3 ft apart, so this is conservative.

    Top and bottom seams

    Bending is 1,200 lb·in per foot of width. The M8 that ties two top and bottom walls sees about 270 lb when the wind pushes and 760 lb when it pulls, 15% of its 4,950 lb proof load. An angle on the seam adds a second tie at the back.

    Side seams

    The pins below set where every block sits, so the side M8s only clamp the seam shut. Even with the steel running up and down, so a side seam carried the full 1,200 lb·in per foot, the two M8s at each station work as a couple 4 3/8 in deep: the worst bolt, on a 2 ft seam with one station, sees about 550 lb, 11% of its proof load, and on a 3 ft seam about 410 lb.

    Side walls over 4 ft

    11 MPa in Rib and 15 MPa in Hoop, 7% and 5% of yield, bending 0.013 in and 0.006 in. The U rail, the back flange and its lip, and Rib's two beads all work with the wall.

    Seam rails over 3 ft

    The plain channels see 20 MPa in Rib (12% of yield) and 25 MPa in Hoop (9%), bending 0.025 in and 0.009 in.

    Hub mounts

    At twice the hub's weight for handling, Rib's hanger shelf sees 35 MPa and moves 0.2 mm, and its three rivets about 140 N each. Hoop's hinge shelf sees 30 MPa and moves 0.2 mm, and the two clinches at its root about 420 N each in tension.

    Base rail

    Carrying a 3 ft row of Hoop blocks, the heavier frame, between seats 5 ft apart, it sees about 2 MPa and sags about 0.3 mm. The steel on the first row line takes over from there.

    Fabrication drawings

    Nineteen A3 sheets for quotation and engineering review: Rib (R-0 to R-6), Hoop (H-0 to H-5) and the parts both frames share (C-1 to C-5), with flat patterns, bills of materials, hardware and the hub mounts. Millimeters, with inches in brackets. Tooling is rated Low, Medium or High on each process.

      Assumptions and open items

      • Rib or Hoop. Rib is the recommended frame to launch, with Hoop as the volume frame. Both are drawn in full so either can be quoted.
      • Cirrus IN and OUT connector. The exact part and its dimensions are still needed. The model and drawings use a 30 mm twist lock on 60 mm centers as a placeholder.
      • Joint torque. Every joint bolt runs in by hand with a 13 mm socket and no impact driver, as on Cirrus frames today. The tightening torque gets set with the nut supplier: PEM nuts in 1.6 mm aluminum on Rib, weld nuts in 1.2 mm steel on Hoop. The base rail M8s are held to 12 N·m until confirmed.
      • Hub heat. The hub now hangs in the open 6 in cavity, with vents in every wall, rather than bolting to the rails through thermal pads. Earlier work had the screen dimming 10 to 15% at peak only with sustained full white in direct sun at about 104 F; that needs a fresh check in a test block.
      • Hoop shelf clinches. Confirm the two clinches at the root of the hinge shelf with a pull test on a clinched sample before the tooling is cut.
      • Tooling and piece cost. Rated Low, Medium or High, Rib against Hoop, for one 3 x 3 frame and its hub mount. Fabricator quotes will set the cost.
      • Other block sizes. Smaller blocks have more frame edge per square foot than a 3 x 3: 1.25 times as much for the 2 x 3 and 3 x 2, and 1.5 times for the 2 x 2.
      • Weights. Panels are taken as 3 lb each and the L hub as 5.5 lb. A full 3 x 3 block comes to 4.9 lb per sq ft on Rib and 6.7 on Hoop, lighter than Daktronics, Watchfire and Optec.
      • Wind. 50 psf is a planning number. The sign company's engineer sets the real one for each site, as both Daktronics and Watchfire require.
      • Logo. Size and position are a first pass from the Cirrus logo. Printed, applied as a decal, or debossed (Hoop) is a later call on look and finish; none of them changes the frame.
      • Hole grid. 7/16 in holes for 3/8 in or M10 bolts. The sign company's engineer sets the bolt size and spacing for each job; the grid only makes sure the holes are there.
      • Reading of the 6 in rule. "Sides at least 6 in wide" is read as 6 in front to back, so the side walls give the joints a deep face and the logo a tall field.
      • Finish. Black polyester powder, RAL 9005. The 3D model shows the frames gray only so the details read.
